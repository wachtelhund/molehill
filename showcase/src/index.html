Molehill

A falling-sand colony sim. You dig and build underground, and every material follows its weight.

Concept illustration, not gameplay — not a screenshot of the game.

Molehill is a falling-sand platformer and colony sim, written in Rust and Bevy. You dig tunnels, place supports, and furnish rooms. Sand slides, water finds its level, and stone fails when a span is too wide. Light has to be placed; a tunnel stays dark until you bring a lamp, a torch, or a material that already glows.

This is an early development build. You can download it or play the web version in your browser.

The ground

Cut tunnels that will hold

You cut a round bite through dirt and stone, then run and jump through the space you made. There is no wall climbing. You roll onto a ledge within reach; a higher one needs a rope ladder or a hook from the rope gun. What you leave behind has to carry its own weight. A short span of dirt holds; a wide hall needs pillars. Brick and iron are ordinary cells, judged by the same numbers as the rock.

You start in a small burrow and dig outward. Shallower ground is softer and wetter. Deeper ground is harder, hotter and richer in ore.

The world

Earth without an edge

The ground goes on in every direction. There are no side walls and no floor to reach: the part of the world the game holds slides along with you, up, down or sideways, and what you leave behind is kept as you left it, tunnels, lights and all. The same seed always gives the same earth.

The rock changes as you go. Warm soil and roots in the Loamlands, pale wet limestone in the Karst caverns, granite and loose gravel in the Granite crags, glowing growth in the Fungal hollows, and basalt and lava deep down in the Ashlands. Their edges wander, so no border runs straight.

Physics

Weight, water, fire, and light

Every cell is a material with density and strength. Sand slides. Water levels out. Lava creeps, and where it meets water it quenches to loose scoria. A charge is a pressure wave: it travels down a tunnel, fades across a cave, and breaks dirt long before granite. When stone cracks, it crumbles in the falling-sand simulation, and the next span takes the load.

There is no day cycle and no sky light. A tunnel stays dark until you place a light or stand near a glowing material.

Play

Survival and Creative

You can furnish rooms with tables, nests, rugs, carved pillars, and painted stone, and light them with lamps you place.

New Holt asks which mode to use. Survival starts with a small kit. You dig with the tools you own, find food, and burn fuel for lamps and torches. When you fall, the pack stays where you died. Creative has every item, and nothing hurts.

Getting around

Ropes, lifts and a borer

The rope gun throws a hook into the rock; you climb its rope hand over hand and swing on it. Rope ladders hang where you set them. A lift cage rides a shaft between stops, balanced with a counterweight of stone.

Further along, a borer you ride cuts its own tunnel. It burns coal or wood and boils the water it carries, and drops the rock it cuts as rubble behind it. Behind that it pulls a train of wagons: stores, a workshop bench, a bunk to sleep in, a water tank and a coal bunker that stokes it.

The Deepholt

No sky, ever

We dig in an endless body of earth. There is no sky and no surface. Nobody has found a top or a bottom.

A Delver makes a holt: a home to prop, furnish, and keep lit. The work is to dig out, bring things home, and light another stretch of wall.

Older marks in the rock seem to point upward, toward a story of open air called the Lid. They prove nothing. The Lid remains a myth.

Everything is handmade and physical. Sand pours because it is loose. Water finds its level because it is water.

Download

Get the development build

  • macOS: universal zip (Apple silicon and Intel)
  • Windows: x86_64 zip
  • Linux: x86_64 tar.gz
  • Play the web version in your browser (keyboard and mouse)

The builds are not signed. On macOS, right-click molehill and choose Open the first time, or run xattr -d com.apple.quarantine molehill. On Windows, if SmartScreen warns you, click More info, then Run anyway.
