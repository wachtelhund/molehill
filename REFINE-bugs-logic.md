# Refine run: bugs and logic

Short log of what this run changed, and what is still open.

## Changed
* **Placed pillars and beams** (the user's bug report: they collapsed and rolled away like powder). I fixed this on this branch, and a parallel run landed an equivalent fix on main first (`6bbb493`). When merging I kept main's solver (`stress.rs`, `mat.rs`) and dropped my overlapping version. My scenarios run against main's solver and pass: `pillar_built_in_room`, `pillar_sand_floor`, `beam_bridge_ledges`, `beam_overload_iron` (wood breaks into splinters, iron holds), and `pillars_rock_roof` with its control `roof_needs_pillar` (pillars rescue a roof that fails in bending).
* Scenario harness: `place` directive (now main's brush placement; the default pillar brush is one cell).
* **TNT no longer explodes where you click** (`logic.rs`, `world.rs`, game `main.rs`). Placing TNT (item or device) puts down an inert charge. Right mouse lights a 1.5 s fuse (it blinks); fire or lava lights it too; water puts it out. Current on an adjacent copperline sets it off at once, which gives remote detonation by lever, knot or thrummer→listener. A blast nearby or a fuse cord burning into it also sets it off at once. `Ctrl`+right mouse on a lit charge defuses it back into the pack. Lava no longer blows TNT instantly (it lights the fuse), and a charge buried under rubble stays put instead of being "destroyed by cave-in".
* **Device state is reset on place/remove**: a fuse cord picked up while burning used to come back already burning; channels, latches and listener/thrummer flags no longer leak to the next device on that block.
* **Pack accounting**: dragging copperline over a knot used to eat a copperline every frame. `Ctrl`+right mouse now refunds the copperline under a device too, and a knot placed on copperline gives the copperline back. Placement goes through `World::place_device / use_device / lay_wire / pick_up`, which also check reach.
* **Save/load**: burning fuses and lit TNT keep burning after a load, and the death count is kept (optional trailing section, so older v3 saves still load). Loading a save where the mole is far below the burrow no longer counts as a fall: it used to kill the mole on load.
* Harness: `device`, `use`, `pickup`, `wire`, `saveload` directives; `dev:`, `lit:`, `on:`, `wire:`, `ev:` variables. Action events (clicks, places) are no longer dropped. New scenarios: `tnt_inert`, `tnt_lever`, `tnt_thrum`, `tnt_defuse`, `tnt_douse`, `saveload_devices`. New tests: `load_deep_does_not_fall_damage`, `wire_and_device_pack_accounting`, `replaced_fuse_is_not_burning`.
* **Doors never shut on the mole**: a door (or floodgate) told to close waits while a mole stands in its block, then closes once it is clear. A door can't be placed on yourself. Scenario `door`.
* Knots audited with a truth-table scenario (`knots`: both, either, turn, odd, catch). They were correct.
* **Pressure plates see the whole mole**: a plate used to check only the mole's feet cell, so a plate just above the floor never triggered (depending on block alignment). It now uses the same body overlap as doors. Scenario `plate`.
* **Lamp and Vent pack items place their device** on left click (they used to do nothing in hand).

* **Hotbar spun by itself (web report)**: the wheel handler used `scroll.delta.y.signum()`, and Rust's `f32::signum(0.0)` is `1.0`, so every frame without scrolling stepped the hotbar (or zoomed while Shift was held). `view::Wheel` now turns scroll into notches: no scroll is no notch, a notched wheel gives one notch per click, and pixel scroll (trackpads, smooth-scrolling browsers) gives one notch per flick, ignoring the momentum tail until the wheel is quiet for 0.12 s. Test `wheel_idle_is_still_and_trackpad_flick_is_one_notch`.
* **Web crash hunt**: the web build now installs `console_error_panic_hook`, so a panic prints its message and line in the browser console instead of a bare `unreachable`. Not reproduced: 4 min of scripted play in headless Chrome (1x and retina sizes, wasm memory flat at 106 MB), a 16-seed random-play soak (`examples/soak.rs`: digging, blocks, devices, wire, blasts, save/load) with overflow checks, and saves from the two previous site builds (`examples/loadsave.rs`) all ran clean. Found and fixed on the way: a debug overflow in the disaster RNG seed; the game now refuses a save whose world size differs from the build instead of indexing out of range. Test `corrupt_saves_never_panic` (truncated and bit-flipped saves load or fail cleanly, never panic).

* **Scrolling while sprinting zoomed** (found by p3): Shift is sprint, but Shift+wheel was zoom. Now the plain wheel always picks a hotbar slot (Shift changes nothing; a Shift-turned sideways wheel still counts), `Ctrl`/`Cmd`+wheel zooms (a trackpad pinch too), and `Alt`/`Option`+wheel sizes the brush. `view::wheel_use` / `wheel_delta`; test `wheel_modifiers_sprint_does_not_zoom`. Controls screen, README, AGENTS.md and wiki updated.

## Phase 2 (p4): chunked save/load for the endless world
* **Builds no longer vanish off-window.** Only cells (material and flags) used to survive the window sliding away. Copperline, devices (and lit fuses), air, torches, drops, stations and mushroom plots in those rows were dropped. The off-window world is now stored as 64-row (8 m) **bands** (`sim/src/band.rs`, `World::bands`), each one blob with all of that. Test `bands::things_survive_leaving_the_window`; scenario `band_keeps_devices` (new harness directive `slide`).
* **Save format 4 = manifest + band blobs.** The manifest holds the globals and the band list (start row, FNV-1a hash, length). `save::save_parts` gives the blobs keyed `b<row>_<hash>`, and the game stores each under its own key (`molehill-auto/b…` in localStorage, or a `molehill-auto/` folder natively). Unchanged bands keep their key, so an autosave writes only the bands that changed (the window, about 0.3 MB, in 3 ms) instead of the whole explored world. Bands go first, the manifest last, then stale keys are removed, so a failed write (storage full) leaves the old save whole; autosave failures now show a message. `save::save`/`load` still give one blob, with the bands inline. Tests `save_keeps_off_window_bands`, `autosave_writes_only_changed_bands`, `missing_or_damaged_bands` (a hole around the mole fails the load; a lost far band is regenerated and logged).
* **Format 3 saves still load**: their row cache becomes bands. Fixture `tests/data/v3_cpm8.bin`, test `v3_save_still_loads`.
* **Load is about 50 ms instead of 20 s**: `World::blank` builds the world shell without generating a window the load would overwrite anyway.
* `examples/bandbench.rs` times slides, autosave, save and load on a game-sized world.

## Interaction rules (Hampus)
* **One table says what an item does**: `Item::uses` (set down / throw / pick up) and `Item::controls` (the input line). The slot tooltip shows that line, and so does the held-item label over the hotbar. Test `item_uses_table_is_consistent`.
* **TNT**: set down unlit with left or right mouse; thrown lit with `Q`. `Q` refuses blocks, copperline, devices, torches and tools ("can't be thrown") instead of throwing anything in hand. Right mouse on an empty spot sets down the device or TNT in hand. Scenario `tnt_place_vs_throw`.
* **Picking up is `E`** (was `Ctrl`+right mouse): the hovered device, unlit TNT, torch, or one copperline segment; hold and sweep for a run. The device on a block comes up first and its copperline stays laid until the next pass. A lit charge or burning fuse can't be picked up (Hampus: unlit only), which replaces the old defuse. World hover tips say "E: pick up". Scenarios `pickup_tnt` (replaces `tnt_defuse`), `pickup_wire`, `pickup_device`; test `torch_picks_up`. The harness gained a `torches` variable.

## Brush size and grid snap (Hampus)
* **Brush size couldn't be changed on a Swedish Mac.** `[` is Option+8 there: the game read the physical key (Å) and the digit picked hotbar slot 8 instead (reproduced in headless Chrome with scripted keys). `[` / `]` now count by the character typed or the US key position, and Option+digit doesn't pick a slot. Option+wheel did work, but the pickaxe's default bite sits one step under its maximum, so growing it looked like nothing happened, and the label said "brush 6.0" in cells. The size is now written beside the outline and over the hotbar, in metres. `view::BrushSizes` holds the rules; test `brush_keys_any_layout`, scenario `brush_size_keys` (keys, Option+wheel, Shift/Ctrl/Cmd+wheel leave it alone, a bigger bite digs more).
* **Grid snap (`X`)** for blocks: squares of 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 m on a fixed grid, stepped by the size keys, with a square outline. A drag lays a straight row or column (`World::place_line`); the outline shows the whole line. Scenario `place_grid_snap`. Harness: `hand`, `sizekey`, `sizewheel`, `snap`, `line`; variables `brush`, `snapcells`.

## Survival crafting (PROGRESSION.md)
* Built on the Survival mode from `refine/survival` (`survival.rs`: kit, food, fuel, death drop), which landed while this was in progress: its `Mode`/`Rules` gained `crafting` (on in Survival). Creative and Plain craft anything anywhere.
* **Stations** are the design's three: workbench (always works), forge (needs lava or fire within 3 m, or a lava-fed heatstone), workshop (needs a live copperline into it). Old kilns and furnaces load as forges, anvils as workshops (`band::station_from_code`). Station art reuses the old kinds (`Station::art`). `World::station_ready`.
* **Recipes unlock by touch**: `World::known` fills from the pack every tick; a recipe is listed once any input is known, and the log names what opened. Save format 6 keeps `known`. Chains are at most two steps (`recipes_are_short_chains`).
* **Tool ladder**: the Pickaxe (workbench), Drill (workshop) and Bore (forge) replace stone/bronze/iron tools; basalt now needs the Bore (tier 3).
* **Crafting list**: pack open at a station lists its recipes down the left (bright = can make, dim = missing something; a header says if the station is cold or unpowered); click to craft. Unowned tools are hidden from the hotbar in Survival.
* Scenarios `craft_unlock_by_touch`, `craft_stations`, `tool_tiers_survival`; tests `recipes_are_short_chains`, `survival_starts_bare_and_learns`. Harness: `switch`, `craft`, `station`, `pour`; variables `known:`, `recipe:`, `tier`, `buildings`.

## Iteration 2: snappy feedback and Survival balance
* **Every action answers on its tick** (sound event + small shake + pop): each dig strike now shakes lightly (stronger with bigger tools); a dig the tools can't do says so at once (a clink, a light shake and "Granite is too hard: it needs a drill.") instead of silence; landing from 0.5 m bumps; a throw is felt (more at full power) with a pop at the paw; picking up, using a device and setting a torch pop. Movement was already instant (run speed in 1 tick, stop in 1). Scenario `feel_feedback`.
* **Balance from a survey** (`examples/survey.rs`: what lies around the burrow per seed). Wood is scarce near spawn (0 to 12 items within 40 m), lava 20 to 34 m away, iron 30 to 38 m in granite, crystal 40 to 51 m. So: the Survival kit carries 6 wood; the workbench costs 4 wood + 4 stone and the workshop 2 wood + 6 stone + 6 copperline; the forge burns a coal (or charcoal) per job when no lava, fire or heatstone is in reach (the crafting header says so), so the first copper needs no lava; the drill is made from 3 copper + 2 tin ingots (it used to need iron, but iron ore needs the drill). Test `survival_ladder_is_reachable` climbs the tool ladder from the kit with what each tier can dig; scenario `forge_coal_fuel`.

## Bronze makes the drill
* Bronze had no use in Survival. The Drill (tier 2, workshop) is now 4 bronze ingots: copper and tin alloyed at the forge, after copper and before iron (and cheaper on tin than the old 3 copper + 2 tin ingots). Bronze can also be co-smelted straight from ore ("Bronze x2": 3 copper ore + 1 tin ore), which keeps the bronze drill within the two-step chain rule. Test `bronze_makes_the_drill` (and no ingot is a dead end); `survival_ladder_is_reachable` now also checks bronze comes before the drill; scenario `craft_stations` alloys bronze and makes the drill from it.

## Open
* The design's emberiron for the Bore doesn't exist, so the Bore takes iron, copper and crystal.
* localStorage holds about 5 MB. A band is 10–40 KB, so a holt explored over a few hundred metres of depth could fill it. Bands the player never changed could be dropped and regenerated (not done: the falling-sand settling after generation would differ).
* The web crash Hampus saw is not reproduced. Next time it happens, the browser console (View > Developer > JavaScript Console) shows `panicked at <file>:<line>`; that line pins it.
* Main's stress model still sums overburden through air gaps (a dirt layer under ~16 m of rock with caves in between is crushed even if the caves carry it). Not a placed-block problem; left as is.
* Placing TNT onto a copperline that is already live sets it off on the next tick. That is consistent (it is powered), but easy to do by accident.
